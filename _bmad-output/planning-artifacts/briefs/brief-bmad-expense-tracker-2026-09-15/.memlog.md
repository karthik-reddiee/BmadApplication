- (event) Create Brief workflow started for bmad-expense-tracker; workspace initialized for interactive discovery.
- (decision) Initial product scope: record, view, update, and delete expenses with amount, date, category, and description; manage categories; show total spending and spending by category for a selected period.
- (assumption) Initial technical direction is React frontend, ASP.NET Core Web API backend, and PostgreSQL database, but these are provisional and should be refined later through BMAD architecture.
- (decision) Product direction: small personal expense tracker for individuals who want simple day-to-day spending visibility without large finance-app complexity.
- (decision) MVP constraints: web application focused on simplicity and fast manual expense entry; first version excludes bank-account integration, account aggregation, and complex financial automation.
- (decision) Project purpose: learning project intended to follow BMAD from discovery through implementation, keeping scope small enough to build the MVP and understand every part of the architecture.
- (decision) Working mode: Coaching path selected so the brief is developed section by section with product reasoning surfaced before drafting.
- (decision) Current alternatives: memory, notes apps, spreadsheets, or no tracking; memory and notes lack structure, while spreadsheets add friction for small expenses.
- (decision) MVP principle: record an expense quickly, then understand spending clearly; avoid features that slow entry or turn the product into advanced personal finance management.
- (decision) Problem trigger: user opens the app immediately after everyday purchases to capture an expense before forgetting, with secondary end-of-day or weekly review.
- (decision) Expense entry happy path: Amount, Category, Date defaulted to today, optional Description, then Save; normal entries should take only a few interactions without multiple screens.
- (decision) MVP expense fields: required Amount, Category, Date; optional Description; exclude mandatory payment method, merchant, location, receipt, account, or tags.
- (decision) Main screen should prioritize quick expense entry, with concise current-period summary and recent expense list visible without navigation.
- (decision) Categories: provide sensible defaults such as Food, Transport, Shopping, Bills, Entertainment, Health, Education, Other, plus simple user-created categories; no nested categories or rules in MVP.
- (decision) Overview period: current month is primary for MVP, showing total spending, spending by category, and recent expenses; custom date ranges are deferred unless later BMAD planning makes them valuable.
- (decision) Primary MVP persona: working professional with regular day-to-day expenses who wants simple financial awareness; product remains broadly usable by students, freelancers, and individuals.
- (decision) Usability target: approachable for basic-to-moderate software familiarity; simple, clear, mobile-friendly, instruction-light, and focused on a small number of actions.
- (decision) MVP user model: single-user only; future architecture may leave room for multi-user or household sharing, but MVP excludes sharing, collaboration, roles, permissions, and multi-user features.
- (decision) Primary motivation: financial awareness, not specific budgeting goals or savings targets; MVP should answer how much was spent, where money went, category totals, and visible patterns.
- (decision) Monthly success: user understands total monthly spend, highest-spend categories, frequent expense types, and changes across weeks or periods; product makes spending visible without prescribing behavior.
- (decision) Project success: complete BMAD lifecycle artifacts and build a working expense tracker with frontend, backend, PostgreSQL integration, clear responsibility boundaries, basic tests, BMAD code review, and no required deployment.
- (decision) User success: record a normal expense in under 30 seconds via amount, category, save, with date defaulted to today and optional description; quickly see current-month total and recent expenses.
- (decision) Off-track rule: if a feature complicates expense recording or basic spending awareness without being essential to the core experience, it belongs outside the MVP.
- (decision) MVP core experience: Record, Review, Understand.
- (decision) MVP core capabilities: add, view, inspect, edit, delete expenses; use default categories; create/select custom categories; view current-month total, category breakdown, recent expenses, and simple selected-period review if it stays simple.
- (decision) MVP excludes bank and credit-card integrations, automatic imports, investments, bills, complex budgeting, goals, forecasting, OCR, auto-categorization, recurring automation, social/household/multi-user features, advanced notifications, complex analytics, AI advice, and payments/transfers.
- (decision) Authentication scope: no real authentication for first MVP; treat as single-user while avoiding architecture choices that make future multi-user support impossible.
- (decision) MVP supports expenses only; income tracking is out of scope because the core problem is understanding spending behavior.
- (decision) Category management scope: users can create, edit, and delete custom categories; default/system categories may be protected; exclude nested categories, hierarchies, rules, and automatic categorization.
- (decision) Long-term vision: evolve from expense tracking to spending insights, lightweight budgeting, and broader personal financial awareness while becoming more useful without becoming more complicated.
- (decision) Differentiation: focus and simplicity, not feature breadth; fast expense recording, minimal setup, no account linking, and focused spending awareness.
- (decision) Product principle: Know where your money went without turning expense tracking into a second job.
- (decision) BMAD and learning are project/development goals, not user-facing product differentiators; user-facing differentiation is simplicity, speed, and focused spending awareness.
- (decision) Future possibilities outside MVP: auth/accounts, income, budgets, recurring expenses, advanced charts/trends, custom date ranges, export/import, mobile app, household sharing, reminders, OCR, auto-categorization, bank sync, and AI-assisted insights.
- (event) Product Brief finalized: executive summary added, structure/prose polish applied, all substantive memlog decisions incorporated into brief; no addendum needed.
